# 07 — Sales Call Playbook

**Call 1 goal:** sell the $2,500 Growth Teardown. Nothing else.
**Call 2 goal (findings session):** present the Teardown and sell the Sprint or retainer.

You are not pitching a retainer on call one. Trying to is the single most common reason
agency calls fail at this price point — you'd be quoting a solution before you know the problem,
which is exactly what every agency that burned them already did.

---

## Before the call — 15 minutes of prep

Non-negotiable. Walking in cold destroys the diagnostic positioning.

- [ ] Browse the store on **mobile**. Note load speed, PDP structure, checkout friction.
- [ ] Meta Ad Library: how many ads live? How long has the top one been running? What angles?
- [ ] Buy something cheap, or at minimum abandon a cart → see if a flow fires.
- [ ] Sign up to the email list → check the welcome flow.
- [ ] Check the founder's LinkedIn: tenure, background, recent posts, any trigger events.
- [ ] Write down **three specific observations** you can name on the call.
- [ ] Form a hypothesis about their primary constraint — and hold it loosely.

---

## Call structure (30 minutes)

| Phase | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Frame | 2 min | Set the agenda and the exit |
| 2. Context | 5 min | Their story, in their words |
| 3. Diagnose | 12 min | The questions that do the work |
| 4. Reflect | 4 min | Say their problem back, better than they said it |
| 5. Prescribe | 5 min | The Teardown, priced |
| 6. Close | 2 min | Book the next step |

### 1. The frame (say this almost verbatim)

> "Thanks for the time. Here's how I'd like to use it: I'll ask about the business and what's
> actually happening with your numbers — that's most of the call. At the end I'll tell you one of
> three things. Either there's an obvious next step with us, or there's something you should go
> do yourself that doesn't need us, or we're not the right fit and I'll tell you who is. All
> three are fine outcomes. Sound alright?"

This does four things: sets the agenda, gives them permission to be honest, makes "no" a
legitimate outcome, and immediately separates you from the last four agency calls they took.

### 2. Context (let them talk)

> "Give me the short version — where's the business at, and what's happening with it right now?"

Then shut up. Take notes. Do not solve anything yet.

---

### 3. The diagnostic question bank

Use maybe 10–12. Follow the energy — where they get uncomfortable or vague is where the money is.

**Situation**
1. What's the business doing in revenue, roughly, and how has that trended over 12 months?
2. What are you spending monthly on paid, and across which platforms?
3. Who's doing what right now — internal, agencies, freelancers?
4. What's your AOV, and what's your gross margin after COGS and shipping?

**Measurement (this is where the real problems surface)**
5. What's your blended MER? *(Hesitation here is the most diagnostic moment in the call.)*
6. When your ad platforms report revenue, how close is that to what Shopify says?
7. What number do you personally look at first thing on a Monday?
8. Do you know your contribution margin per order?

**Problem**
9. What's the single thing that, if it were fixed, would make the most difference?
10. When did growth actually stall? What changed around then?
11. What have you already tried? What happened?
12. If you had to guess where the biggest leak is — acquisition, conversion or retention —
    what's your gut say?

**Implication (make the cost real, using their numbers)**
13. What does this cost you if nothing changes in 12 months?
14. What does it cost you personally? Time, headspace, weekends?
15. What are you not able to do because this isn't sorted?

**Need-payoff**
16. If your blended MER improved by half a point, what would that be worth to you annually?
17. What would you do with the capacity if you weren't the one connecting all these vendors?

**Decision process**
18. If we decide this makes sense, who else needs to be in that conversation?
19. Have you set a budget for this, or are you working out whether it's worth one?
20. What's your timeline — is there something forcing the date?

**The permission question (ask it, always)**
21. "Is there any reason you *wouldn't* want to fix this?"

---

### 4. Reflect it back

The moment the call is won or lost. Say their problem back to them more clearly than they said it.

> "Let me play this back. You're at [$X]M, spending [$Y] a month. You've got an ads agency, a
> freelancer on email and a developer, and every one of them reports good numbers. But your
> blended MER has gone from [A] to [B] over the last year, and profit is flat while revenue is up.
>
> The thing I'd note is that no one you've described is accountable for the number you actually
> care about. Your ads agency can hit their ROAS target while your conversion rate drops, and
> they've technically done their job — and you've made no more money.
>
> That's not four problems. It's one problem showing up in four places. And right now the only
> person connecting them is you. Does that land, or have I got it wrong?"

Then **stop talking.** If they say "yes, exactly" — you've sold it. Everything after this is
logistics.

---

### 5. Prescribe

> "Here's what I'd suggest, and it's deliberately not a retainer pitch.
>
> I don't quote before I know. Everyone who's pitched you has done the opposite, and that's part
> of why you're where you are. So we'd start with a Growth Teardown — $2,500, one week.
>
> We go through Shopify, your ad accounts, Klaviyo and GA4, and you get: every leak we find
> sized against your own numbers and ranked, a model of what your revenue and contribution
> margin look like if the top three get fixed, a 90-day roadmap, and a recorded session where I
> walk you and your team through all of it.
>
> If I don't find three specific, quantified opportunities, you don't pay.
>
> If you take that roadmap and run it internally, honestly that's a good outcome and I'll tell
> you so. If you want us to run it, the $2,500 comes straight off your first invoice.
>
> What's your reaction to that?"

### 6. Close

> "The Teardown takes a week from when we get access. I've got capacity starting [date] — want
> me to send the access list and the invoice today?"

Book the findings session **before you hang up**. A booked calendar slot is worth ten follow-ups.

## Objection handling

Format: **acknowledge → reframe → evidence → return the question.**

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Fair. Compared to what, though — what you're paying your current vendors monthly, or the
> cost of another year of this? What's the current stack costing you all-in each month?"

**"Why would I pay for an audit when others are free?"**
> "Because a free audit is a sales pitch with a spreadsheet attached. It'll find whatever
> problem matches what they sell. This is a week of senior time producing something you can act
> on with or without me — and because you've paid for it, I'm not incentivised to find a
> convenient answer."

**"We've been burned by agencies before."**
> "I'd assume that, at your stage. What went wrong? … Right. So the structural fix here is that
> we start with a $2,500 diagnostic instead of a retainer, agree baselines in writing, and the
> retainer is rolling after month six. I've built every step so you can stop. That's not
> generosity — it's the only way I get to be believed."

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Of course. What specifically do you want to think through — whether the problem's real,
> whether we're the right people, or whether now's the right time?"
> *(Almost always one of those three. Handle the actual one.)*

**"Can you just do the ads?"**
> "I could, but I'd be taking your money for something I don't think is your constraint. If ads
> were the problem, changing agency would have fixed it. Happy to point you to a good media
> buyer if that's genuinely what you want."

**"Can you guarantee results?"**
> "No, and I'd be careful with anyone who does — it depends on your stock, your pricing, your
> product decisions, things I don't control. What I guarantee is the Teardown finds three real
> opportunities or you don't pay, that we agree a baseline in writing, and that I report against
> it honestly at day 90 whether it moved or not. That's what I can actually stand behind."

**"We're too busy right now."**
> "Understood. Is it a bandwidth problem or a priority problem? Because the Teardown needs about
> two hours of your time total, and the point of it is to make you less busy, not more."

**"Send me a proposal."**
> "Happy to — but proposals are usually where good conversations go to die. What would need to
> be in it for you to say yes? Let's work that out now and I'll send exactly that."

## The findings session (call 2)

**Order matters. Do not present the roadmap first.**

1. **Restate their goal** in their words. (2 min)
2. **Present findings, worst first.** Every leak with the number attached, from their data. (20 min)
3. **The model.** What the top three fixed is worth, assumptions visible on the slide. (5 min)
4. **The roadmap.** Sequenced, honest about effort. (10 min)
5. **The fork.** "You can run this yourself — here's what you'd need. Or we can run it. Both
   are legitimate. Which do you want to talk about?" (5 min)
6. **If they choose you:** scope, price, start date, agreement out same day. (10 min)

**The line that closes Sprints:**
> "You've now got a document that's worth more than what you paid for it either way. The only
> question left is who executes it and how fast."

---

## Call hygiene

- Talk **less than 40%** of the time on call one. If you're above that, you're pitching.
- Never present a projection as a prediction. Say "on your numbers, with these assumptions."
- Never cite a benchmark you can't source. "I don't know, I'd have to check" is a sales asset.
- Never discount to close. Reduce scope instead.
- Disqualify out loud and early. It's the most persuasive thing you'll do all call.
- Send a written recap within 2 hours: what you heard, what you proposed, what happens next.

## Post-call checklist

- [ ] Written recap sent (their problem in their words, the proposal, the next step)
- [ ] CRM updated: score, trigger event, objection raised, next action + date
- [ ] Next call booked, or a specific follow-up date set
- [ ] If disqualified: referral sent, and added to the long nurture list
