# 05 — Lead Magnet

## The strategic role

Your paid Growth Teardown ($2,500) is the real conversion event. The lead magnet exists to
create demand *for that*, from founders who aren't ready to book a call today.

It must do three jobs:
1. **Make the invisible problem visible** — they see leaks they didn't know they had.
2. **Prove competence by being useful on its own** — they get value without ever speaking to you.
3. **Make the Teardown the obvious next step** — because self-scoring shows them the gaps, and
   the gaps need someone to fix.

A lead magnet that requires you to be present to deliver value has failed. This one shouldn't.

---

## The magnet: **The eCommerce Growth Leak Audit**

**Format:** a 40-point self-scored diagnostic (PDF or interactive page) + a 12-minute walkthrough
video where you score a real, anonymised store live.

**Positioning line:** *Find out where your store is leaking revenue — in 20 minutes, without
talking to anyone.*

**Why a scorecard and not an ebook:** an ebook is read once and forgotten. A scorecard makes them
*do* something, produces a personalised result, and creates the gap that your offer closes. The
score is the hook; the gap is the sale.

---

## Structure — 40 points across 5 sections

Scored 0 (not in place) / 1 (partly) / 2 (fully in place). **Max 80.**

### Section 1 — Measurement (8 points)
1. You can state your blended MER for last month from memory.
2. Your reported ad platform revenue reconciles to Shopify within 15%.
3. Server-side tracking is implemented and verified.
4. You know your contribution margin per order, after COGS, shipping and ad spend.

### Section 2 — Acquisition & creative (16 points)
5. You test 5+ genuinely new creative concepts (not variations) per month.
6. Creative briefs are built from performance data, not opinion.
7. You know which single ad drove the most new-customer revenue last month.
8. Your top-spending ad is less than 90 days old.
9. You have more than one profitable acquisition channel.
10. You know your new-customer CAC separately from your blended CAC.
11. Your ad account structure was reviewed in the last 90 days.
12. You have a documented creative testing cadence anyone on the team could run.

### Section 3 — Conversion (16 points)
13. You know your site conversion rate on mobile separately from desktop.
14. Mobile pages load in under 3 seconds on a real 4G connection.
15. Your top 3 PDPs have been tested (not just redesigned) in the last 6 months.
16. Shipping cost and delivery timing are visible before checkout.
17. You offer more than one payment method beyond cards.
18. You run at least one structured conversion test per month.
19. Your PDP answers the top 5 objections a buyer actually has.
20. You've watched session recordings of real users in the last 90 days.

### Section 4 — Retention & email (20 points)
21. Welcome, abandoned cart, browse abandon and post-purchase flows are all live.
22. Those flows have been revised in the last 6 months.
23. You know what percentage of revenue comes from email/SMS.
24. You know your repeat purchase rate.
25. You have a replenishment or reorder sequence.
26. You send more than 4 campaigns a month.
27. You segment by purchase behaviour, not just engagement.
28. Your list is sunset-managed for deliverability.
29. Less than half your email revenue depends on a discount code.
30. You have a defined win-back sequence for lapsed customers.

### Section 5 — Economics & offer (20 points)
31. You know your AOV and have deliberately tried to raise it.
32. You have a bundle, tier or kit offer live right now.
33. You have a post-purchase upsell.
34. You know your 90-day and 12-month customer LTV.
35. You have a non-discount offer that drives volume.
36. Your free shipping threshold is set above AOV, deliberately.
37. You know which product drives the most *second* orders.
38. Your margin by product is documented and current.
39. You've raised prices in the last 18 months.
40. You know what you can afford to pay to acquire a customer, and stay under it.

---

## The scoring page (where the sale is made)

**Under 30 — Fragmented.** Most of your growth is currently being bought, not earned. The
opportunity here is large, and it's mostly foundational: measurement first, then conversion.
The good news is that scores in this range usually improve fastest.

**30–50 — Functional but leaking.** The basics exist and nothing is connected. This is the most
common score for a $2–8M brand, and it's the most frustrating place to be: everything works a
bit, nothing compounds. The gains here are in sequencing, not in adding more.

**51–65 — Strong, with specific gaps.** You're doing most things well. Your remaining upside is
concentrated in two or three specific places, and finding *which* ones is worth more than
general improvement everywhere.

**66+ — Advanced.** You're running a genuinely good operation. You probably don't need a growth
partner — you need a specialist for a specific constraint. Tell them that. Saying "you don't
need us" to the highest-scoring readers costs you nothing and buys you enormous credibility.

**CTA on every band:**
> Want us to score your store properly — with your actual account data, and the leaks sized in
> dollars? That's the Growth Teardown. `Book a call →`

## Delivery sequence

| # | Timing | Content | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Instant | Deliver the audit + walkthrough video | Fulfil the promise immediately |
| 1 | Day 1 | "The most common answer to question 24" — repeat purchase rate, why almost nobody knows theirs, why it changes what you can spend | Prove insight |
| 2 | Day 3 | The Blame Loop — why four vendors produce four green dashboards and one flat P&L | Reframe the problem |
| 3 | Day 5 | A real teardown, anonymised: here's what we found in a $[[X]]M store and what it was worth | Demonstrate the method |
| 4 | Day 8 | "Why we charge for audits" — the honesty piece | Differentiate, pre-frame price |
| 5 | Day 12 | Who we say no to | Qualify and repel |
| 6 | Day 16 | Direct offer: book a Teardown | Convert |
| — | Ongoing | Weekly value email, one idea each | Long nurture |

Every email plain-text, from the founder's address, one idea, one link.

## Production checklist

- [ ] Write the 40 questions into a scored format (Typeform, ScoreApp, or a simple HTML page)
- [ ] Record the 12-minute walkthrough on a real anonymised store — with written permission
- [ ] Design the PDF version for people who won't do an interactive quiz
- [ ] Build the 6-email sequence in Klaviyo
- [ ] Landing page: headline, the promise, 3 bullets, email capture. Nothing else.
- [ ] Test that the score result page is shareable — founders forward these to their team

## Honesty guardrails

- The walkthrough video must use a **real store with documented permission**, or a clearly
  labelled composite. Never present a constructed example as a real client.
- Any dollar figure in the nurture sequence must be from your own client work, with the
  measurement method stated. No "brands typically see..." claims.
- The scorecard bands are a **qualitative framework**, not a validated instrument. Don't
  describe the score as a benchmark or imply industry-standard scoring.
