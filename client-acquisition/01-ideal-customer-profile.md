# 01 — Ideal Customer Profile

> **Positioning anchor:** Neon Mountain is an eCommerce Growth Partner. We connect creative,
> acquisition, conversion and retention into one growth system. We sell commercial improvement
> with execution attached — not execution alone.

---

## 1. The one-line ICP

**Founder-led or lean-team Shopify brands doing $2M–$8M/year who are already spending
meaningfully on paid acquisition, have a fragmented vendor stack, and have watched their
blended margin get worse even as revenue stayed flat or grew.**

The $1–10M band is the outer boundary. The **$2M–$8M core** is where the offer lands hardest:
below $2M there is rarely budget or enough traffic for CRO/retention work to move a number;
above $8M brands usually have an in-house growth lead who buys narrower, specialist scopes.

## 2. Firmographics

| Attribute | Target |
|---|---|
| Annual revenue | $2M–$8M (accept $1M–$10M) |
| Monthly revenue | ~$150k–$650k |

| Geography | AU and NZ primary; US/UK secondary (timezone-tolerant clients only) |
| Tenure | 3–8 years trading. Past product-market fit, into the messy scaling middle. |

## 3. The buyer

**Primary economic buyer: the founder.** In this band the founder still signs off on anything
over ~$3k/month and still has opinions about the brand. They are the decision maker and the
main blocker.

**Secondary influencer: the marketing manager / eCommerce manager.** Often the person who
first finds you, forwards your content, and has to not feel threatened by you. Treat them as
an ally, never as someone you are replacing.

### Founder psychographics
- Operator, not a marketer. Learned marketing by necessity, knows enough to be dangerous.
- Has been burned by at least one agency. Expects to be over-promised to.
- Time-poor. Reads content on their phone at night, buys in the morning.
- Measures success in bank balance and blended MER/ROAS, not platform ROAS.
- Wants *one throat to choke*. Sick of coordinating four vendors who each blame the others.
- Quietly worried they have hit a ceiling they personally cannot break through.

### What they say out loud
- "Our ads used to work."
- "I don't actually know what's working any more."
- "Everyone shows me great platform numbers but my P&L says otherwise."
- "I'm doing three people's jobs."
- "I don't want another agency, I want someone who owns the outcome."

## 4. Trigger events (when they become buyable)

Rank outreach and content targeting by these. A brand hitting two or more is in-market **now**.

1. **Margin compression** — revenue flat or up, profit down. The single strongest trigger.
2. **Agency breakup** — just fired, or about to fire, their ads agency. Contract ending.
3. **A plateau of 2+ quarters** — spend increases stopped producing revenue increases.
4. **Key person left** — the marketing manager who "knew everything" resigned.
5. **A replatform or site redesign** — new site, conversion rate did not improve, or got worse.
6. **Raised or reinvested capital** — a mandate to scale and a deadline attached to it.
7. **A big seasonal miss** — BFCM or a peak season underperformed against plan.
8. **Rapid SKU or category expansion** — the marketing system was never rebuilt to match.
9. **iOS/attribution wobble** — reported ROAS and actual revenue diverged and nobody can explain it.

## 5. Qualification

### Green flags (pursue hard)
- Spending $30k+/month on paid and can name their blended MER.
- Founder is in the conversation from the first call.
- More than 30% of revenue from returning customers, or an obvious untapped list.
- They already tried to fix it internally and it didn't work — they know it's structural.
- Willing to give access to Shopify, ad accounts and Klaviyo without a fight.

### Yellow flags (proceed with conditions)
- Under $1.5M revenue → the audit may be the only appropriate sale for now.
- Marketplace-dependent (Amazon/eBay > 50% of revenue) → less of the journey is ours to fix.
- A committed in-house team that just needs hands → they want a contractor, price accordingly.
- Wholesale-dominant → the DTC lever we pull is smaller than it looks.

### Red flags (disqualify — say no)
- Wants performance-only pricing with no retainer. Not a partner, a lottery ticket.
- Cannot or will not share data. If we can't measure it, we can't be accountable for it.
- Sub-20% gross margin. There is no room to buy growth.
- Wants "just ads" or "just a website" and is shopping on price alone.
- A history of five agencies in three years. The constant is not the agencies.
- Founder won't attend calls after onboarding.
- Expects results in under 60 days.

> **Write your disqualifiers into the sales page.** Telling a founder who you *don't* work with
> is the fastest trust-builder available to an agency, and it kills bad-fit calls before they
> waste an hour.

## 6. Where they actually are

- **Instagram / TikTok** — following brand-building and eCom operator accounts. Consumes,
  rarely comments. Reachable by content, not by pitch.
- **LinkedIn** — where they behave like a business owner rather than a brand. Best channel for
  the "profit, not platform ROAS" argument. Highest-intent inbound DMs come from here.
- **Email** — reads on mobile early morning. Cold email works if it names a specific,
  observable problem on *their* store.
- **Peer networks** — private founder groups, mastermind chats, Slack/WhatsApp communities.
  Word of mouth in this band is disproportionately strong.
- **Shopify ecosystem** — app partners, 3PLs, accountants and Klaviyo/agency partner networks
  are underrated referral sources.

## 8. Anti-ICP

Explicitly not for: pre-revenue and pre-product-market-fit brands, dropshippers, print-on-demand,
one-product stores with no repeat mechanic, brands under 20% margin, anyone who describes their
problem as "we just need more content", and anyone whose core ask is a single deliverable at the
lowest available price.
