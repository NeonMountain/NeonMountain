# 06 — Content & Outreach

Channels selected: **Instagram + TikTok**, **LinkedIn**, **Cold email / DM outreach**.

Content builds the demand. Outreach harvests it. Run both — outreach alone gets ignored,
content alone is too slow when you need clients this quarter.

---

## Part A — Content

### The one idea everything ladders back to

> **Fragmented marketing is why growth stopped. Connected marketing is why it restarts.**

Every post should be traceable to that sentence. If it isn't, it's content for other agencies,
not for founders.

### Five content pillars

| Pillar | % of output | Job | Example angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Diagnose** | 30% | Name a problem they have and can't articulate | "Your ads aren't broken. Your creative pipeline is." |
| **2. Teach** | 30% | Give away a usable method | "How to calculate what you can actually afford to pay for a customer" |
| **3. Contrarian** | 20% | Attack a comfortable industry belief | "Platform ROAS is the most expensive number in eCommerce" |
| **4. Proof** | 10% | Show the work — real, permissioned | Anonymised teardown of a real store |
| **5. Human** | 10% | Founder POV, how you operate, who you decline | "We turned down $8k/month last week. Here's why." |

**Ratio discipline:** most agencies invert this and post 70% proof and promotion. Diagnose and
Teach are what get shared to the marketing manager, and the marketing manager is how you get to
the founder.

---

### 30 content angles (ready to write)

**Diagnose**
1. Your ads didn't stop working. They aged out, and nothing replaced them.
2. Four vendors, four green dashboards, one flat bank balance.
3. You don't have a traffic problem. You have a conversion problem you're paying to hide.
4. If your platform revenue and Shopify revenue disagree by 30%, every decision you made this
   quarter was made on fiction.
5. The reason your redesign didn't work: you changed how it looks, not how it sells.
6. Discounting isn't a strategy. It's the symptom of not having one.
7. You're paying full CAC for the same customer twice.
8. The bottleneck in your marketing is a person, and it's you.
9. Your best-performing ad is 14 months old. That's not a win, that's a warning.
10. Nobody in your marketing stack owns the number that pays your wages.

**Teach**
11. How to work out what you can actually afford to pay to acquire a customer.
12. The four flows that should exist before you send another campaign.
13. Reading blended MER instead of platform ROAS: a 5-minute explainer.
14. Why your free shipping threshold should sit above your AOV, not below it.
15. A creative testing cadence a two-person team can actually run.
16. The five objections your PDP has to answer, in order.
17. How to set a baseline before you change anything (and why almost nobody does).
18. Segmenting by purchase behaviour instead of open rate.
19. Post-purchase upsells: the cheapest revenue in eCommerce.
20. How to brief creative from data instead of from a moodboard.

**Contrarian**
21. Platform ROAS is the most expensive number in eCommerce.
22. Most CRO is redecorating.
23. You don't need more content. You need the content you have pointed at something.
24. Hiring in-house at $3M is usually a mistake. Here's the maths.
25. Free audits are sales pitches with a spreadsheet attached.
26. AI won't fix your marketing. It'll help you do the wrong thing faster.

**Proof / human**
27. What we found in a $[[X]]M store in one week (anonymised, permissioned).
28. We charge for audits. Here's why that's better for you.
29. The brands we say no to, and the reason.
30. What we got wrong on a client engagement, and what we changed.

### Format by channel

**Instagram + TikTok** — the visual proof channel. You're a creative agency; the content is
the portfolio.
- **Screen-record teardowns (30–60s).** You, scrolling a real store, calling out three
  conversion problems. Single highest-performing format for this business. Permission or
  anonymise, always.
- **Whiteboard/talking head (20–45s).** One idea, no intro. Hook in the first 1.5 seconds.
- **Carousel frameworks.** The Blame Loop, the 4-pillar model, the audit scorecard. Highly saved,
  highly forwarded.
- **Before/after creative.** Only your own work, only with permission.
- Rules: burned-in captions, hook on screen as text within 1s, no logo intro, no music-led edits.

**LinkedIn** — the argument channel. Highest-intent inbound for a $5–10k/mo offer.
- Text posts, 120–250 words, one idea, line breaks every 1–2 sentences.
- Lead with the diagnose and contrarian pillars. The "profit vs platform ROAS" argument is your
  strongest LinkedIn asset.
- One document/carousel post a week — LinkedIn still favours them.
- Comment substantively on 5–10 ICP-adjacent posts daily. In this band, comments generate more
  qualified DMs than posts do.
- Never post a link in the body. Put it in the first comment or ask people to comment a keyword.

**Cadence (sustainable, not aspirational)**
| Channel | Frequency | Weekly effort |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 4 posts/wk + daily commenting | ~3 hrs |
| Instagram/TikTok | 3 videos + 2 carousels/wk | ~4 hrs |
| Email list | 1 email/wk | ~1 hr |

**Repurposing chain — write once, ship five times:**
`One idea → LinkedIn post → 45s video script → IG carousel → weekly email → a section of the audit`

## Part B — Outreach

### The list

Build 200 qualified accounts before sending anything. Sources:
- Shopify/Shopify Plus store directories and BuiltWith-style tech lookups
- Brands actively running ads in the **Meta Ad Library** — filter for ones running the same
  creative for 90+ days (a visible, nameable problem)
- Recent Klaviyo, Recharge and Gorgias adopters (they're investing in the stack)
- Local eCom awards lists, trade show exhibitor lists, retail media features
- Second-degree LinkedIn connections in the founder's own network

**Per-account research (5 minutes, non-negotiable):** find one specific, observable thing —
an ad that's been running unchanged for months, a PDP with no reviews above the fold, a mobile
load problem, no post-purchase flow (test by buying something cheap). **The specificity is the
entire strategy.** Generic outreach at this price point does not work.

---

### Cold email sequence

**Subject lines (lowercase, specific, no hype):**
- `your [product] PDP`
- `noticed something — [brand]`
- `[brand] + a conversion thing`

**Email 1 — the observation**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I was looking at [Brand] this morning — [specific observation, e.g. "your hero ad in the Meta
> library has been running unchanged since March"].
>
> Usually when I see that it means creative production has become the bottleneck, not the media
> buying. It's the most common reason spend stops scaling at your stage.
>
> I record short teardowns for brands like yours — 5 minutes, three specific things I'd change,
> no pitch. Want me to send one over?
>
> [Your name]
> Neon Mountain

**Email 2 (day 4) — the mechanism**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Following up on the above. The pattern I see most in $2–10M Shopify brands: an ads agency, an
> email freelancer, a developer, a content person. Everyone's metrics are green. The P&L is flat.
>
> Nobody owns the connection between them, so gains in one place get cancelled out in another.
>
> Still happy to record that teardown if it's useful.
>
> [Your name]

**Email 3 (day 9) — value, no ask**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Last one from me. Either way, here's the 40-point audit we use to score a store's growth
> system — it's free and you don't need us to run it: [link]
>
> Most brands at your stage come out in the 30–50 range, which is the frustrating middle:
> everything works a bit, nothing compounds.
>
> Good luck with [specific thing — new launch, season, campaign].
>
> [Your name]

**Email 4 (day 20) — the breakup**

> Hi [Name] — closing the loop on this. If timing's wrong, no problem at all. If it changes,
> I'm here. [Your name]

**Rules:** 20–30 sends/day per inbox, warmed domain, plain text, no images, no tracking pixels,
no attachments. Reply-to a real monitored inbox. Never send from your primary domain.

---

### LinkedIn DM sequence

Connect first with **no note**. Higher accept rate. Then:

**DM 1 (day 2 after accept):**
> Thanks for connecting [Name]. Not pitching — I look at a lot of $2–10M Shopify brands and
> [Brand] came up. Curious: is creative production or conversion the bigger constraint for you
> right now?

**DM 2 (day 6, if they reply):**
> That tracks. [Specific, useful response to what they said — give real value here, this is
> where the relationship is made or lost.]

**DM 3 (day 10, if warm):**
> Want me to record a 5-minute teardown of your store? Three things I'd change, no pitch, no
> obligation. Happy to just send it over.

The free 5-minute video teardown is the highest-converting outreach asset available to you.
It is the single most repeatable move in this entire document.

---

### Instagram DM

Only after they've engaged with your content. Cold IG DMs to founders don't work.
> Hey [Name] — saw you [engaged with the teardown post]. I do those for stores properly, free,
> 5 mins, no pitch. Want one for [Brand]?

## Weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | Write the week's core idea. Ship LinkedIn post 1. Add 40 accounts to the list. |
| Tue | Record 2 videos. Send outreach batch 1 (40). LinkedIn post 2. |
| Wed | Record 2 teardown videos for warm prospects. Follow-ups. LinkedIn post 3. |
| Thu | Carousel + weekly email. Outreach batch 2 (40). Calls. |
| Fri | LinkedIn post 4. Pipeline review. Nurture replies. Plan next week. |
| Daily | 20 min LinkedIn commenting. Clear the inbox. |

## Metrics to track weekly

| Metric | Target to aim at | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts added to list | 200/wk | Leading indicator of everything |
| Emails sent | 400/wk | Across warmed inboxes |
| Positive replies | — | Track your own rate, then optimise it |
| Teardown videos recorded | 5/wk | Your highest-leverage activity |
| Calls booked | 3–5/wk | The only number that predicts revenue |
| Audits downloaded | — | Nurture pool size |

> These are activity targets to hold yourself to, not predicted conversion rates. Establish your
> own baselines over the first 6–8 weeks and set realistic targets from your actual data — not
> from benchmarks you read somewhere.
