## SECTION 3 — Reframe (the mechanism)

## Growth isn't a channel problem. It's a system problem.

Most brands at your stage try to grow by adding: another agency, another channel, another
freelancer, more spend. It doesn't work, because the constraint isn't capacity. It's that
nothing is connected.

When creative, acquisition, conversion and retention are run separately:

- Creative gets briefed on opinions instead of data.
- Media buying optimises to a platform number that doesn't reconcile to your P&L.
- Your site is judged on how it looks instead of how it sells.
- Email waits for a sale to have something to say.
- And every gain in one place is quietly cancelled out somewhere else.

**A growth system means one strategy, one team, one number.** What we learn in ads changes the
site. What converts on the site changes the email. What retention tells us changes what we're
willing to pay to acquire. That compounding is the entire point — and it is impossible to get
from four vendors who have never spoken to each other.

## SECTION 9 — FAQ

**How is this different from the agency we've already got?**
Most agencies own a channel. We own the number. If your ads agency improves ROAS while your
conversion rate drops, they've hit their target and you've made no more money. We're accountable
for blended performance across the whole journey, which means we have nowhere to hide.

**Why do you charge for an audit when everyone else gives one away?**
Because a free audit is a sales pitch with a spreadsheet attached. A paid one takes about a week
of senior time and produces something you can act on with or without us. Charging for it also
means we're not incentivised to find whatever problem happens to match what we sell.

**What if we just want the ads managed?**
Then we're probably not right for you, and there are good specialists we can point you to. We
only take on work where we can affect the whole journey — otherwise we can't be accountable for
the result.

**Do we have to stop working with our current vendors?**
Not necessarily. Sometimes the answer is to keep a great freelancer and change what they're
briefed on. The Teardown will tell you.

**Who actually does the work?**
A named senior lead, from the first call onward. You will not be handed to a junior account
manager after the sale.

**How fast will we see results?**
The first 30 days are usually tracking and foundations, which feels slow and is the reason
everything after it works. Meaningful movement typically shows in the second and third month.
Anyone promising you a transformation in six weeks is selling you something.

**What if it doesn't work?**
The Teardown is guaranteed. The Sprint has agreed baselines reported honestly at day 90. The
retainer is rolling after six months with 30 days' notice. We've structured every step so you
can stop, and so we have to keep earning it.

## Build notes

**Structure:** long-form single page. Hero CTA, mid-page CTA after the offer section, final
CTA. Three total, all identical in wording and destination.

**Booking:** straight to a calendar embed. Every field on the form is a conversion cost — ask
only for name, email, store URL, monthly revenue band and monthly ad spend. Those five let you
qualify before the call.

**Above the fold on mobile:** headline, subhead, CTA. Nothing else. 70%+ of your traffic is
mobile.

**Voice:** direct, specific, unhyped. No superlatives, no "10x", no rocket emojis. The
credibility of this page comes entirely from precision.

**Before publishing:**
- [ ] Every `[[ ]]` filled with verified data or the section deleted
- [ ] Guarantee wording reviewed against your terms of engagement
- [ ] Client permission in writing for every quote, logo and metric
- [ ] Prices confirmed against delivery margin
- [ ] Page speed under 3s on mobile — a slow page selling CRO is a credibility problem
