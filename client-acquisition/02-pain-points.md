# 02 — Pain Points

The job of this document is to let you describe a founder's problem better than they can
describe it themselves. That is the entire mechanism of trust in this market.

**The master pain:** *Their marketing is a set of disconnected parts, and nobody — including
them — owns the number that actually matters.*

Every pain below is a symptom of that one structural fault. Neon Mountain's whole argument is
that these are not four problems with four vendors. They are one problem.

---

## 1. The four surface pains (by pillar)

### Pillar 1 — Acquisition
| Surface pain (what they say) | Underlying cause | Cost to them |
|---|---|---|
| "Our ads used to work and now they don't." | Creative fatigue and too few new concepts tested per month | CAC drifts up quietly, month after month |
| "Platform ROAS looks fine but I'm not making money." | Reported vs incremental attribution gap; agency optimises to the dashboard | Decisions made on numbers that don't reconcile to the P&L |
| "We're scared to increase spend." | No reliable creative pipeline, so spend increases just buy more impressions of tired ads | Growth ceiling, self-imposed |
| "Our agency keeps asking us for more creative." | Creative production sits outside the media buyer's control | Bottleneck no one owns |

### Pillar 2 — Conversion (Shopify / CRO)
| Surface pain | Underlying cause | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| "We get traffic but it doesn't convert." | PDP and offer clarity, not design; mobile load and friction | Every acquisition dollar underperforms |
| "We just redesigned and it didn't help." | Redesign was aesthetic, not hypothesis-driven | Spent real money, changed nothing |
| "Checkout abandonment is brutal." | Shipping cost surprise, payment options, trust gaps | Losing customers already paid for |
| "Mobile is our worst converting device." | Site built desktop-first; mobile is 70%+ of traffic | The majority of traffic gets the worst experience |

### Pillar 3 — Retention & Email
| Surface pain | Underlying cause | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| "We only email when we have a sale." | Campaign-only, no flow architecture | Discount-trained list, margin erosion |
| "Klaviyo is set up but I don't think it's working." | Flows built once at launch and never revisited | The cheapest revenue in the business, ignored |
| "Repeat rate is lower than it should be." | No post-purchase or replenishment sequencing | Buying customers once, at full CAC, forever |
| "Our list is big but unengaged." | No segmentation or sunset strategy | Deliverability decays, then everything decays |

### Pillar 4 — Economics (AOV & margin)
| Surface pain | Underlying cause | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| "We can't afford to spend more to acquire." | AOV never engineered — no bundles, tiers, or upsell path | CAC ceiling far lower than it needs to be |
| "Discounting is the only thing that moves volume." | No non-discount offer architecture | Trains the market, destroys margin |
| "Revenue is up but profit isn't." | Growth bought at negative contribution margin | Working harder for less |

## 2. The five pains they never say out loud

These are the ones to write content about. Naming an unspoken pain is what makes a founder
stop scrolling.

1. **"I don't actually know what's working."**
   They have four dashboards that disagree. They make six-figure decisions on a feeling and
   are quietly ashamed of it.

2. **"I've become the bottleneck in my own business."**
   Every vendor needs a decision from them. They are the integration layer, and they know
   they're bad at it.

3. **"I think I've hit the limit of what I personally know how to do."**
   The skills that got the brand to $2M are not the skills that get it to $8M. Admitting that
   feels like admitting failure.

4. **"I'm paying a lot of people and I'm not sure any of them are accountable."**
   Four invoices. Four sets of green metrics. One flat bank balance. Nobody has ever said
   "that was my fault."

5. **"If this doesn't turn around, I don't know what plan B is."**
   The genuine fear underneath. Do not exploit this one — acknowledge it with respect. Fear-based
   selling to a stressed founder will win a bad client and lose your reputation.

## 3. The Blame Loop

Draw this on a whiteboard on the sales call. It closes deals because it describes their
actual experience.

```
        Ads agency: "The site doesn't convert."
                  ↓
        Dev/CRO:   "The traffic quality is bad."
                  ↓
        Email:     "We only get sent the leftovers."
                  ↓
        Creative:  "Nobody briefs us on what's working."
                  ↓
        Founder:   sits in the middle, holds all the context,
                   has no time, and owns all the risk.
                  ↓
        → back to the top, every month
```

**The line to use:** *"You don't have four problems. You have one problem showing up in four
places. And right now the only person connecting them is you."*

## 4. What they've already tried (and why it failed)

Knowing this lets you pre-empt objections before they're raised.

| They tried | Why it failed | Your reframe |
|---|---|---|
| Hiring a better ads agency | Ads were never the constraint | "Changing the driver doesn't fix the engine" |
| A site redesign | Aesthetic, not conversion-led | "You changed how it looks, not how it sells" |
| Hiring in-house | One person can't cover four disciplines | "You hired a generalist to solve four specialist problems" |
| A cheaper offshore team | Execution capacity, zero strategy | "More output pointed in no particular direction" |
| Discounting harder | Bought revenue, sold margin | "You solved a growth problem with a profit problem" |
| An expensive consultant | Great deck, no execution | "Diagnosis without treatment" |
| More content | Volume without a conversion path | "You made more, you didn't make more *sell*" |

## 5. Cost of inaction

Use these as *frameworks the prospect fills in with their own numbers on the call.* Do not
attach industry averages you cannot source.

- **The CAC drift.** "If CAC rises X% a year and AOV stays flat, at what point does paid
  acquisition stop being profitable for you? Let's do that maths now, with your numbers."
- **The retention gap.** "What's your current repeat purchase rate? What would one extra
  order per customer per year be worth at your AOV and margin?"
- **The conversion gap.** "At your traffic volume, what does a 0.3 percentage-point conversion
  rate improvement equal in annual revenue?"
- **The fragmentation tax.** "Add up what you pay all vendors monthly. Now add the hours you
  personally spend coordinating them. That's the real cost of the current setup."

> **Honesty rule:** every number in these conversations must come from the prospect's own
> account data or your own documented client results. Never quote a benchmark you cannot cite,
> and never present an estimate as a projection.

## 6. Pain → message map

| Pain | Content angle | Offer component that resolves it |
|---|---|---|
| Don't know what's working | "Your dashboards disagree. Here's the only number that doesn't lie." | Growth Audit + single-source reporting |

| Traffic doesn't convert | "You don't have a traffic problem. You have a conversion problem you're paying to hide." | Shopify/CRO sprint |
| Only revenue is discounted revenue | "Discounting isn't a strategy. It's a symptom." | Offer & AOV architecture |
| Repeat rate too low | "You're paying full price for the same customer twice." | Email & retention system |
| Too many vendors | "Four vendors. Four dashboards. One person doing integration: you." | One partner, one system, one number |
| Personal bottleneck | "The business is now bigger than the way you're running it." | Growth partner model |
