The mechanism

Growth isn't a channel problem. It's a system problem.

Most brands at your stage try to grow by adding: another agency, another channel, another freelancer, more spend. It doesn't work, because the constraint isn't capacity. It's that nothing is connected.

When creative, acquisition, conversion and retention are run separately, creative gets briefed on opinions instead of data. Media buying optimises to a platform number that doesn't reconcile to your P&L. Your site is judged on how it looks instead of how it sells. Email waits for a sale to have something to say. And every gain in one place is quietly cancelled out somewhere else.

A growth system means one strategy, one team, one number. What we learn in ads changes the site. What converts on the site changes the email. What retention tells us changes what we're willing to pay to acquire. That compounding is the entire point — and it is impossible to get from four vendors who have never spoken to each other.

Questions

The ones we get asked most.

How is this different from the agency we've already got?

Most agencies own a channel. We own the number. If your ads agency improves ROAS while your conversion rate drops, they've hit their target and you've made no more money. We're accountable for blended performance across the whole journey, which means we have nowhere to hide.

Why do you charge for an audit when everyone else gives one away?

Because a free audit is a sales pitch with a spreadsheet attached. A paid one takes about a week of senior time and produces something you can act on with or without us. Charging for it also means we're not incentivised to find whatever problem happens to match what we sell.

What if we just want the ads managed?

Then we're probably not right for you, and there are good specialists we can point you to. We only take on work where we can affect the whole journey — otherwise we can't be accountable for the result.

Do we have to stop working with our current vendors?

Not necessarily. Sometimes the answer is to keep a great freelancer and change what they're briefed on. The Teardown will tell you.

Who actually does the work?

A named senior lead, from the first call onward. You will not be handed to a junior account manager after the sale.

How fast will we see results?

The first 30 days are usually tracking and foundations, which feels slow and is the reason everything after it works. Meaningful movement typically shows in the second and third month. Anyone promising you a transformation in six weeks is selling you something.

What if it doesn't work?

The Teardown is guaranteed. The Sprint has agreed baselines, reported honestly at day 90. The retainer is rolling after six months with 30 days' notice. We've structured every step so you can stop — and so we have to keep earning it.
