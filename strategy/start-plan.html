Three clients in 90 days. Then hire.

You are the product right now, and that's fine — it's how every agency starts. The job for the next 90 days is narrow: prove you can sell it, deliver it once brilliantly, and find the ceiling where you need a second pair of hands.

What you sell, in one sentence

I find the money leaking out of your eCommerce business, then I fix it.

Not ads. Not websites. Not email. The diagnosis, then the repair — and the proof that it works is a $3.4M brand I run myself, where I found every one of the problems below.

Your unfair advantage

SHE STREET is a better case study because it went wrong.

Every agency leads with a win. You can lead with something far more credible: a real $3.4M brand, run by you, that was quietly bleeding — and a list of exactly what was broken, found in hours.

Founders don't believe polished case studies. They believe someone who says "I made these mistakes in my own business, with my own money, and here's how I caught them."

The plan

90 days, four moves.

Week 1–2~10 hrs total

Become sellable. Not polished — sellable.

Done when: someone can find you, believe you, and book a call.

  • Write the SHE STREET teardown up as a one-page case study. The table above, plus what you'd do about each. Half a day.
  • Put the sales page live — it's already built. Swap the placeholder proof for the case study and add a Cal.com link. One day.
  • Set your prices and don't move them. $2,500 Teardown. $6,000/month retainer to start. Write them down.
  • Set up the entity and a business bank account. Boring, one afternoon, blocks nothing later.
  • Make a list of 20 people you already know who run or work at eCom brands. Names on paper.

Week 3–6~8 hrs/week

Ten free teardown videos. Your network first.

Done when: 10 videos sent, 3+ calls booked.

  • Record a 5-minute screen teardown for ten brands off your list. Three specific things you'd fix. No pitch, no deck, no obligation.
  • Start with the accounts you already touch — HE STREET, Easy Lift Georgia, Topp Group. Warm relationships convert first and fastest.
  • End every video the same way: "If you want me to do this properly, with your actual account data, that's the Teardown. $2,500, one week."
  • Post two of them publicly (permission first, or anonymised). This is your entire content strategy for now.
  • Don't build a lead magnet. Don't build a funnel. Ten videos to ten real people beats both.

Week 7–10~12 hrs/week

Sell two Teardowns. Deliver them brilliantly.

Done when: $5,000 collected and two findings sessions delivered.

  • Never quote before the Teardown. It's the whole differentiator and it's the thing no competitor does.
  • Require ad account access and COGS up front. You learned this the hard way on your own audit — it was weaker without them.
  • Over-deliver on the first two. These are your external case studies. Treat them as marketing spend, not margin.
  • Ask for the quote at the findings session, while they're still looking at what you found. Not a month later.
  • Pitch the retainer in the same meeting. "You can run this roadmap yourself, or I can run it. Both are fine. Which do you want to talk about?"

Week 11–13~15 hrs/week

Convert one to a retainer. Find your ceiling.

Done when: one retainer signed and you know your real capacity.

  • Convert at least one Teardown into a $6k/month retainer. One is enough. It proves the ladder works.
  • Track your actual hours for the whole quarter, by task. You'll be wrong about where they go — everyone is.
  • Write down what you did twice. Anything repeated is the first thing to templatise or hand over.
  • Keep selling Teardowns while you deliver. The classic solo mistake is stopping sales to do the work, then starving a month later.

Who to hire first

Not a designer, and not an account manager. Hire the person who executes what you diagnose — a media buyer or a Shopify/Klaviyo implementer, contract first, part-time. You stay the diagnosis and the client relationship; they become the hands. That's the only split that works while you're still the one with the knowledge.

Discipline

What not to do.

Everything on this list will feel productive and will cost you the quarter

  • Don't build the Growth Monitor yet. It's a good idea for month 9, not month 1. Clients first.
  • Don't build a lead magnet, a nurture sequence, or a content calendar. Ten teardown videos to ten real people beats all of it.
  • Don't redesign the brand. You're an art director; this will eat weeks and win you nothing.
  • Don't do cold outreach yet. Your network is warmer and faster. Cold is for when warm runs out.
  • Don't discount the first client to land them. It sets your price ceiling permanently.
  • Don't take a client who won't give you data access. You cannot be accountable for what you cannot measure.
  • Don't hire before client #3. Hiring into uncertain revenue is how solo agencies die.

Straight talk

What I'm assuming, and what I don't know.

Check these before you commit

  • The money figures are capacity maths, not forecasts. They assume 15 hrs/week, a $2,500 Teardown and a $6,000 retainer. I don't know your available hours or what you can charge in your market — track one month and replace them.
  • I dropped the retainer to $6,000 from the $6–10k band you picked earlier, because solo delivery is the constraint. Charge more if you can sell it; just know a $10k retainer solo is roughly 35–40 hours a month.
  • I'm assuming your network has eCom brands in it. The whole Week 3–6 move depends on that. If the list of 20 is actually a list of 5, cold outreach moves forward and the timeline stretches.
  • Publishing the SHE STREET numbers is your call. They're real and they're yours, but they're also candid about your own business. Anonymise if that's more comfortable — it loses some power and keeps most of it.
  • Three clients in 90 days is a goal, not a prediction. I have no basis to tell you it's likely. It's achievable with the network advantage; it is not guaranteed.
