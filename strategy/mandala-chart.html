Get out of execution. Build the team. Run the agency.

You are currently five people at SHE STREET: ads, marketing, art direction, creative execution and eCom management. Neon Mountain cannot grow while one person holds all five. Every action below moves a hat off your head or puts a client in the pipeline — sixty-four of them, each small enough to start this week.

Out of hands-on execution, with a team that delivers without me, and $50k/month recurring across 6+ clients — by 30 September 2027.

Six retainers at $6–10k/month — SHE STREET can be one of them, at full rate. Your role reduced to three things: strategy, review, and the client-facing call.

Centre block holds the goal and the eight pillars. Each outer block expands one pillar into eight actions. Scroll sideways on a narrow screen — full detail is in the cards below.

Expanded

Eight pillars, sixty-four actions.

Pillar 1

Positioning & Proof

  1. Paper the SHE STREET caseSigned permission to publish the teardown — even though you own it. Sets the precedent for every client after.
  2. Publish Case Study 0159% of revenue untagged, $8,715 at 1.72 ROAS identified, the pixel find. Exact numbers, method shown.
  3. 3 teardowns you don't ownOwned-brand proof isn't market proof, and buyers know it.
  4. Lock the one-liner“We build eCommerce growth systems — and we watch them.” Stop rewording it.
  5. 3 written client quotesWith permission to publish. Chase them at day 90, not later.
  6. Ship “What your AI can't see”The bot-traffic find, anonymised. Nobody else can make this piece.
  7. Real proof or noneThe sales page ships with verified numbers or that section gets deleted.
  8. Publish the disqualifiersWho you say no to. Converts better than any testimonial.
Pillar 2

Offer & Product

  1. Cost the tiers for realHours × rate against actual delivery — including the team you're about to build.
  2. Pick one guaranteeOne risk reversal you can honour, lawyer-reviewed. Not four options.
  3. Access becomes mandatoryAd account + COGS before a Teardown starts. SHE STREET's was weaker without them.
  4. Templatise the TeardownDocumented end to end so a team member can run it, not just you.
  5. Build Growth Monitor v1Scheduled diagnostic across Shopify, Klaviyo, Meta.
  6. 60-day Monitor trialRun it on SHE STREET. Log every flag, true and false.
  7. Price it once it earns itIt sells when it catches something you didn't already know. Not before.
  8. Cap Teardowns per monthSet the number now, while saying no is still easy.
Pillar 3

Demand Generation

  1. Build 200 qualified accountsMeta Ad Library, Shopify directories. Five minutes of real research each.
  2. Warm a separate domainBefore a single send. Never your primary domain.
  3. 4 LinkedIn posts / weekFrom the 30 angles. Diagnose and contrarian first.
  4. 5 teardown videos / weekFree, five minutes, three specific fixes. Highest-converting asset you have.
  5. Ship the 40-point AuditScored and shareable. The score is the hook; the gap is the sale.
  6. 6-email nurture livePlain text, your address, one idea each.
  7. 3 videos + 2 carousels / weekYour art direction, finally pointed at your own business.
  8. 8 weeks before targetsMeasure your own baselines. Don't import benchmarks you can't source.
Pillar 4

Sales System

  1. Learn the frame verbatimThe opening and the reflect-back, word for word until it's yours.
  2. Never quote before diagnosingNo exceptions. It's the entire differentiator.
  3. Book call two before hanging upA booked slot beats ten follow-ups.
  4. Recap within 2 hoursTheir problem in their words, the proposal, the next step.
  5. A CRM with four fieldsScore, trigger event, objection raised, next action + date.
  6. Hold price, cut scopeDiscounting invoice one sets the ceiling forever.
  7. Disqualify out loudEarly, and to their face. Most persuasive move in the call.
  8. 3–5 calls booked / weekThe only activity number that predicts revenue.
Pillar 5

Delivery Engine

  1. Standardise the tracking fixLeak 01 at SHE STREET. It will be leak 01 almost everywhere.
  2. One reporting templateBlended MER and contribution margin. Built once, reused.
  3. SOP each audit pillarAcquisition, conversion, retention, economics — written down, not in your head.
  4. Baselines in writing, week 2Signed off by the client before anything changes.
  5. Report day 90 honestlyWhether it moved or not. This is the whole reputation.
  6. Weekly 30-min decision callWith the founder. A decision meeting, not a status update.
  7. Client keeps everythingAccounts, assets, dashboards, SOPs in their name. No hostages.
  8. Cap concurrent engagementsDelivery quality is the constraint, not demand.
Pillar 6

Team & Handover

  1. List all five hatsAds, marketing, art direction, creative execution, eCom management. One person wearing all of them is the actual problem.
  2. Rank by “who could do this at 80%?”Hand over the easiest first. 80% done by someone else beats 100% done by you.
  3. Lead designer owns art directionEnd to end. You approve, you stop producing.
  4. Creative producer runs executionOff your briefs. You write the brief, they make the work.
  5. Media buyer owns the accountDay to day. You review weekly against blended MER.
  6. Write the brief templateThe document that lets someone direct without you in the room.
  7. One role handed over per quarterNamed successor, written handover, a date. Four quarters, four hats gone.
  8. Your job: strategy, review, the callNothing else. Write that down and check yourself against it.
Pillar 7

Money & Structure

  1. Separate entity and booksOwn ABN, own account, own P&L for Neon Mountain. Day one.
  2. SHE STREET pays full rateIt becomes client #1, not a favour. Mates' rates hide a broken model.
  3. Cost the team before pricingPrice for the designers and buyer you need, not the solo operator you are.
  4. 6 months runway firstBefore you reduce any income. Non-negotiable.
  5. Blended margin, monthlyPer client. The number that says whether the model works.
  6. No client over 30%Including SHE STREET. Concentration turns a bad month into an ending.
  7. Lawyer the contractsGuarantee wording and engagement terms, before the first external client.
  8. Set a drawing and hold itA number you pay yourself. Don't dip past it to feel safe.
Pillar 8

Founder & Focus

  1. Track two weeks of hoursBy role, before deciding anything. You'll be wrong about where it goes.
  2. Set the target splitWhat % is Neon Mountain, what % is SHE STREET oversight. Write the number.
  3. 3 protected days / weekNeon Mountain only. In the calendar, defended like a client call.
  4. Stop executing creativeBrief it, review it, don't make it. This is the hardest one on the chart.
  5. Art direction = approvalNot production. Your taste is the asset; your hands are the bottleneck.
  6. Say no to everything elseNot client growth or team-building? It's a no.
  7. Weekly: what did I do that they should have?Name it. Then hand that specific thing over.
  8. Quarterly: kill, don't addCut the action that isn't working. Never bolt on a ninth.

How to run it

The chart is worthless if it's only read.

Weekly
Pipeline review, same time, non-negotiable. Pick three actions for the week — never more.
Monthly
Mark what actually moved. Count the blanks honestly; they're the signal.
Quarterly
Kill the action that isn't working. Never add a ninth to a pillar — replace.
The order
Pillar 6 first, then 1 and 7. Hand over a hat, build the proof, separate the books. Selling hard before you have capacity just buys yourself a worse job.

Where I'm working from assumptions, not facts

  • The central goal is a proposal, not a forecast. $50k/month, six clients and September 2027 are numbers I chose to be specific enough to act on. I have no visibility into your runway, your living costs, your team size or what SHE STREET needs from you — all of which change the date. Rewrite the centre cell before you use the chart.
  • I revised this chart mid-build once you said you want to phase out of execution rather than cut ties. Pillars 6, 7 and 8 were rewritten: SHE STREET stays, as a full-rate client, and the detachment is from the work rather than the company. Tell me if that's still off.
  • I don't know your team. Pillar 6 assumes you have designers who can absorb art direction and a path to a media buyer. If you have one junior designer, the quarterly handover pace is wrong and the hiring actions come first.
  • The Growth Monitor in Pillar 2 does not exist yet. Actions 5–7 build and test it before selling it. Don't sell it before it catches something you didn't already know.
  • One teardown is not validation. SHE STREET is a real proof point and a brand you own — which is exactly why Pillar 1 action 3 exists.
  • The Mandala (Mandal-Art) method itself is a well-known goal-setting grid, popularised in sport. I'm confident about the format; I'd avoid repeating any specific claim about its origin or effectiveness without checking a primary source.
