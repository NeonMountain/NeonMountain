Start the agency. Three clients. Then a team.

Four or more paying clients, around $25k/month, and the first hire made — by October 2027.

Centre block holds the goal and the eight pillars. Each outer block expands one pillar into eight actions. Scroll sideways on a narrow screen — full detail is in the cards below.

Expanded

Eight pillars, sixty-four actions.

Pillar 1

Proof & Story

  1. Write the SHE STREET case studyThe honest one. What was broken, the real numbers, what you'd do about each.
  2. Lead with what went wrongA $3.4M brand quietly bleeding is more credible than any win. Nobody believes polished.
  3. Publish the recovery endingIt taxes brands, it doesn't kill them. That's the line most founders need to hear.
  4. 2 external case studiesFrom the first two paid Teardowns. Brands you don't own.
  5. 3 written client quotesWith permission to publish. Ask at day 90, not later.
  6. Post 2 teardown videos publiclyPermission first, or anonymised. This is your content strategy for now.
  7. Replace the placeholder proofSales page ships with real numbers or that section gets cut.
  8. Publish who you say no toDisqualifiers convert better than testimonials.
Pillar 2

Offer & Price

  1. $2,500 and $6,000. Written down.Teardown and monthly retainer. Decide once, stop relitigating.
  2. Don't discount the first clientIt sets your ceiling permanently. Cut scope instead.
  3. Ad account + COGS before startingYou learned this on your own audit. It was weaker without them.
  4. One guarantee you can honourTeardown finds three real opportunities or they don't pay.
  5. Cost your own hours firstTrack a month before you change a price in either direction.
  6. Raise price after case study #3Not before. Proof earns the increase.
  7. Cap at 2 Teardowns a monthWhile solo. Set the number now, while saying no is easy.
  8. Never quote before the TeardownThe entire differentiator. No exceptions.
Pillar 3

Pipeline

  1. List 20 people you already knowNames on paper. eCom founders, marketers, anyone close to a store.
  2. Start with accounts you touchHE STREET, Easy Lift Georgia, Topp Group. Warm converts first and fastest.
  3. Ten free teardown videosFive minutes, three specific fixes, no pitch. Weeks 3–6.
  4. Same one-line ask every time“If you want this properly, with your real data — that's the Teardown.”
  5. Keep selling while deliveringStopping sales to do the work is how solo agencies starve in month three.
  6. Cold outreach only when warm runs outNot before. Your network is faster and converts better.
  7. One post a week from real findingsNot a content calendar. One idea you actually found.
  8. Track which source made each callEight weeks of that tells you where to spend your time.
Pillar 4

Selling

  1. Diagnose on call oneAsk, don't pitch. Talk less than 40% of the time.
  2. Ask for the Teardown in the videoEnd of the five minutes, every time. Make it the habit.
  3. Pitch the retainer at the findings sessionWhile they're still looking at what you found. Not a month later.
  4. Reflect it back better than they said itThe moment the sale is won. Then stop talking.
  5. Recap within 2 hoursTheir problem in their words, the proposal, the next step.
  6. Hold price, cut scopeEvery time. No exceptions for the first client.
  7. Disqualify out loudEarly, to their face. Most persuasive thing you'll do.
  8. Book the next step before hanging upA booked slot beats ten follow-ups.
Pillar 5

Delivery

  1. Over-deliver on the first twoThey're your external case studies. Marketing spend, not margin.
  2. Fix tracking first, every clientIt was leak 01 at SHE STREET. It will be leak 01 almost everywhere.
  3. Baselines in writing firstSigned off before you change anything.
  4. One reporting templateBlended MER and contribution margin. Build once, reuse forever.
  5. Report day 90 honestlyWhether it moved or not. This is the whole reputation.
  6. Weekly 30-min decision callWith the founder. A decision meeting, not a status update.
  7. Client keeps everythingAccounts, assets, dashboards in their name. No hostages.
  8. Write down anything you did twiceRepetition is the signal for what to templatise or hand over.
Pillar 6

Team

  1. Hire at client #3Not before. Hiring into uncertain revenue is how solo agencies die.
  2. First hire is executionNot a designer, not an account manager. Hands, not overhead.
  3. Media buyer or Shopify/Klaviyo implementerWhichever is eating more of your delivery hours.
  4. Contract and part-time firstProve the work exists before you take on fixed cost.
  5. You keep diagnosis and the clientThat's the part only you can do right now. Don't delegate it yet.
  6. Write the brief template firstBefore the hire. It's what makes someone else useful on day one.
  7. Templatise the TeardownDocumented end to end so it isn't locked in your head.
  8. Second hire only when the first is fullCapacity follows revenue, never the other way round.
Pillar 7

Money & Admin

  1. Entity and bank account, week oneBoring, one afternoon, blocks everything if you skip it.
  2. Separate books from day oneOwn P&L for Neon Mountain. You cannot judge it mixed in.
  3. SHE STREET pays full rateIf it's a client, it's a client. Mates' rates hide a broken model.
  4. 50% up front on every TeardownStandard, fair, and it filters out the unserious.
  5. Track hours by task for a monthYou'll be wrong about where they go. Everyone is.
  6. Blended margin per client, monthlyThe number that says whether the model actually works.
  7. Contracts and guarantee reviewedBefore the first external client signs, not after.
  8. Don't spend it before it recursOne retainer is not a salary. Three months of it might be.
Pillar 8

Focus & Pace

  1. Fixed hours, same days weekly15 hrs/week is the plan. In the calendar, not in your head.
  2. Protect the hours, don't exceed themThis runs alongside SHE STREET. Overreaching burns both.
  3. Sell during delivery weeksThe single hardest discipline on this chart, and the one that matters most.
  4. Don't redesign the brandYou're an art director. This will eat weeks and win nothing.
  5. Don't build the Monitor yetGood idea, month nine. Clients first.
  6. One new thing at a timeNo lead magnet while you're building the pipeline. Finish, then add.
  7. Weekly: did I sell anything?One question, every week. If the answer is no twice running, fix that first.
  8. Monthly: mark it, kill itWhat moved, what didn't. Cut the dead action — never bolt on a ninth.

How to run it

The chart is worthless if it's only read.

Weekly
One question: did I sell anything this week? Then pick three actions — never more.
Monthly
Mark what actually moved. Count the blanks honestly; they're the signal.
Quarterly
Kill the action that isn't working. Never add a ninth to a pillar — replace.
The order
Pillars 1, 7 and 3 first — proof, the boring admin, then pipeline. Pillar 6 (Team) does not start until client #3 exists.

Where I'm working from assumptions, not facts

  • I rewrote this chart. The first version assumed you needed to detach from SHE STREET and phase out of execution. Neither is true, so the centre goal and pillars 6, 7 and 8 were rebuilt around starting and growing instead.
  • The centre numbers are a proposal, not a forecast. 4+ clients and ~$25k/month assume a $6,000 retainer and one execution hire. I don't know your available hours, your market's price tolerance or your runway. Rewrite the centre cell with your own numbers.
  • I'm assuming your network contains eCom brands. Pillar 3 depends on it. If the list of 20 is really a list of 5, cold outreach moves forward and the timeline stretches.
  • This chart and the 90-day start plan must agree. The start plan is the first quarter of this chart. Change one and the other needs changing too.
  • The Mandala (Mandal-Art) method is a well-known goal-setting grid. I'm confident about the format; I'd check a primary source before repeating any specific claim about its origin or effectiveness.
